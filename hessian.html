Hessian eigenvectors during training, interactively

A companion to Characterizing optimizer-dependent training dynamics through Hessian eigenvector displacement and localization, with the layer-resolved curvature analysis of LANCE. Pick an objective, a small network and a set of optimisers; the page trains them from the same initialisation, computes the full curvature matrix at regular checkpoints and tracks how its leading eigenvectors drift, localise and align with the gradient and the update. Everything runs locally in your browser.

1 · Objective

Changing the problem loads sensible defaults for the network and optimisers below.

600 handwritten digits from the UCI/scikit-learn 8×8 set (60 per class), centred; 80% train, 20% test. Pooling to 4×4 keeps P small, as the 14×14 pooling does for MNIST in the reference script.

Gaussian inputs, targets from a random one-hidden-layer tanh teacher plus noise; 256 train and 64 test points.

Hard-constrained ansatz u(x) = x(1−x)·NN(x) on (0, 1), so the loss is the mean squared PDE residual only, normalised by the RMS of f. Exact solutions: u* = x(1−x)sin(2πx) for Poisson and x(1−x)sin(6πx) for Helmholtz, as in LANCE.

Adds (ℓ₂/2)‖W‖² to the loss, which shifts the weight block of the curvature by ℓ₂.

2 · Network

ReLU has a zero second derivative, so a PINN with ReLU cannot represent u″. Use tanh, softplus or sin.

3 · Optimisers

All runs start from the same weights and see the same mini-batches. GN-CG and LANCE are damped Gauss–Newton methods and need a least-squares objective.

Halve η every N steps reproduces the step-decay schedule of the reference script (0 keeps η fixed). PINN problems always train full batch.

Gauss–Newton settings

Each iteration solves (G + λI)d = ∇L with K conjugate-gradient steps, backtracks along d (Armijo) and adapts λ (×0.7 after a full step, ×1.5 otherwise). LANCE preconditions CG with exact blocks on layers whose curvature density ρ_ℓ exceeds 1 and the mean diagonal elsewhere.

4 · Curvature

Every checkpoint forms the full P × P matrix on the first curvature samples training points (2P gradient evaluations for the Hessian) and diagonalises it exactly. Baseline inits sets how many independent random networks define the decorrelation level τ (0 skips it).

What is computed

A multilayer perceptron with parameters \(\theta\in\mathbb R^P\) is trained on a loss \(L(\theta)\). At every checkpoint the page forms the curvature matrix \(C\) on a fixed set of samples and diagonalises it exactly, \(C=\sum_i\lambda_i v^{(i)}v^{(i)\top}\) with \(\lambda_1\ge\dots\ge\lambda_P\). Eigenvectors are tracked by index, and their sign is fixed so that \(v^{(i)}\cdot\nabla L\le0\), as in the reference script.

Curvature

Hessian. Column \(j\) is a central difference of the exact (reverse-mode) gradient, $$H_{:,j}=\frac{\nabla L(\theta+\varepsilon e_j)-\nabla L(\theta-\varepsilon e_j)}{2\varepsilon},$$ then symmetrised. That costs 2P gradients per checkpoint.

Gauss–Newton. For a least-squares loss \(L=\|r(\theta)\|^2\) with Jacobian \(J=\nabla_\theta r\), \(\nabla^2L=2J^\top J+2\sum_i r_i\nabla^2 r_i\). The Gauss–Newton matrix \(2J^\top J\) drops the second term, is positive semidefinite and coincides with the Hessian at an interpolating solution.

Both matrices are diagonalised by Householder tridiagonalisation followed by the implicit QL algorithm, in double precision.

Eigenvector statistics

Two-time displacement, sign-invariant: $$\Delta^{(i)}(t_w,t_w+t)=\tfrac1P\min\big\{\|v^{(i)}(t_w)-v^{(i)}(t_w+t)\|^2,\ \|v^{(i)}(t_w)+v^{(i)}(t_w+t)\|^2\big\}=\tfrac2P\big(1-|\cos|\big).$$ The random baseline \(\tau^{(i)}\) is the mean displacement between the \(i\)-th eigenvectors of independently initialised networks.

Localisation: \({\rm IPR}(v)=\sum_j v_j^4\), between \(1/P\) (spread evenly) and 1 (one coordinate).

Alignment with the gradient and with the update: $$a^{(i)}_{\rm grad}=\frac{|v^{(i)}\cdot\nabla L|}{\|\nabla L\|},\qquad a^{(i)}_{\rm upd}=\frac{|v^{(i)}\cdot(\theta_{t+1}-\theta_t)|}{\|\theta_{t+1}-\theta_t\|}.$$ Adam rescales each coordinate and mixes past gradients into the step, so its update can leave the sharp directions almost untouched even when the current gradient points along them.

Layer-resolved curvature

With \(P_\ell\) parameters in layer \(\ell\) (weights and biases), the block trace, energy share and curvature density are $$\tau_\ell=\sum_{j\in\ell}C_{jj},\qquad E_\ell=\frac{\tau_\ell}{\sum_{\ell'}\tau_{\ell'}},\qquad \rho_\ell=\frac{E_\ell}{P_\ell/P}.$$ \(\rho_\ell>1\) means the layer carries more than its share of the curvature. LANCE uses this to choose which diagonal blocks of the Gauss–Newton matrix to treat exactly.

Optimisers

  • SGD: \(\theta\leftarrow\theta-\eta\nabla L_B\).
  • Adam: \(\beta_1=0.9,\ \beta_2=0.999,\ \epsilon=10^{-8}\), with bias correction.
  • SAM: gradient taken at \(\theta+\rho\,\nabla L_B/\|\nabla L_B\|\), then an SGD step.
  • GN-CG: \(K\) conjugate-gradient steps on \((J^\top J+\lambda I)d=\nabla L\), Armijo backtracking from the unit step, damping ×0.7 after a full step and ×1.5 otherwise.
  • LANCE: GN-CG preconditioned by \(M={\rm blkdiag}\big([J_\ell^\top J_\ell+\lambda I]_{\ell\in S},\ [(\mu_\ell+\lambda)I]_{\ell\notin S}\big)\), with \(S=\{\ell:\rho_\ell>1,\ P_\ell\le512\}\) and \(\mu_\ell\) the layer's mean diagonal. Here the residual Jacobian is small enough to form explicitly; the method itself only needs Jacobian–vector products.

PINN residuals

Input derivatives are propagated forward through the network: for a layer \(z=Wh+b\), \(h'=\sigma'(z)\odot z'\) and \(h''=\sigma''(z)\odot z'^2+\sigma'(z)\odot z''\). With the hard constraint \(u=x(1-x)\,{\rm NN}(x)\) the residual \(r_i=\big(u''(x_i)+k^2u(x_i)-f(x_i)\big)/(\|f\|_{\rm rms}\sqrt N)\) is an ordinary differentiable function of \(\theta\), so its gradient, Jacobian and Hessian follow from reverse mode.

Limits of this page

  • Full matrices cost \(O(P^2)\) memory and \(O(P^3)\) time, so the page stops at P = 700. The paper's MLP has about 90,000 parameters and uses Lanczos for the top eigenpairs instead.
  • Finite-difference Hessians are accurate to roughly \(\varepsilon^2\); with ReLU the kinks add noise.
  • Runs are single seeds; repeat with a few seeds before reading much into one curve.
