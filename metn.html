Maximum-entropy temporal networks, interactively

A companion to Maximum entropy temporal networks. Load a temporal network, carve out a subset, fit time-layer × mark-layer models of the form \(\lambda_{ij}(t)=\varphi_{r(i,j)}(t)\,w_{ij}\), and compare them by held-out likelihood, goodness-of-fit diagnostics, raster plots and animated dynamics. Everything runs locally in your browser; no data leaves your machine.

Every setting below says where it enters the analysis; the Fitting tab shows each step on the current data.

1 · Dataset

Synthetic presets have known ground truth (blocks, η, β, reply rates), so you can check that the fits recover it. Your own data: any edge list of source, target, timestamp.

P(reply)

P(repeat)
offspring is i→j again (repetition)
P(broadcast)
offspring is i→k from the same sender

Other offspring draw a fresh block-structured pair. The timeline is exactly Hawkes; reply, repeat and broadcast deliberately break the time × mark factorisation, so the motif analysis has something to find.

Same-origin paths (files in this repository) always work; other sites must allow CORS.

2 · Subset

Keeps only events in this slice of the full time span. Every fit, table and plot uses this subset.

Top-N keeps the N most active nodes and the events among them; mark layers are n × n matrices, so large networks need N ≤ 500. Max events keeps the first N events of the window.

The first share of events, in time order, fits every model. The rest is the test window, used for held-out likelihoods and motif forecasts.

Hawkes likelihoods need distinct times: jitter spreads tied events (one e-mail to many recipients) uniformly within one clock tick. Duplicates are identical (source, target, time) rows.

3 · Estimation

Bins of the piecewise-constant NHPP time layer; each bin's rate is count ÷ width. More bins track cycles in training, add parameters and cannot forecast.

Louvain modularity resolution on the symmetrised training counts; higher γ gives more, smaller blocks. Blocks set the block-pair constraints of Block-DWCM and Block-Mask and the block views.

Every mark distribution is mixed with the uniform one, p → (1−κ)p + κ/|S|, so pairs never seen in training keep a finite test likelihood. It changes scores, not fitted weights.

4 · Featured model

The featured model is simulated in Diagnostics, Motifs, Rasters and Dynamics and shaded in the likelihood tables. Reciprocity adds a reply kernel: each i→j raises the rate of j→i.

Independent runs of the fitted model behind every band, error bar and test-window forecast. More runs are smoother and slower.

Time layers

\(\ell_{\rm time}=\sum_k\log G(t_k)-\int G\,dt\). Test likelihoods condition Hawkes intensities on the full training history. The NHPP cannot extrapolate, so on the test window it falls back to the mean training rate. KS: time-rescaling test on training inter-event times (pooled across senders for per-sender layers).

Mark layers

\(\ell_{\rm mark}=\sum_k\log p_{i_kj_k}\) with \(p_{ij}=w_{ij}/W\) (joint, paired with global time layers) or \(p_{j\mid i}=w_{ij}/\sum_{j'}w_{ij'}\) (conditional on sender, paired with per-sender layers). \(\mathbb E[U]=\sum_{i\neq j}(1-e^{-Kp_{ij}})\) is the expected number of distinct directed pairs.

Full model: test log-likelihood per event

\(\ell=\ell_{\rm time}+\ell_{\rm mark}\). The best cell is highlighted, and the featured model is shaded. With ME-Strength, the global-Poisson and per-sender-Poisson rows coincide on training data: this checks the time × mark factorisation.

Full model: training ΔAIC / ΔBIC

Reciprocity extension

Each event \(i\to j\) raises the intensity of the reverse pair: \(\lambda_{ij}(t)=c\,G_{r(i,j)}(t)\,p_{ij}+\rho\,\beta_\rho\sum_{t_k\lt t,\;m_k=j\to i}e^{-\beta_\rho(t-t_k)}\), so \(\rho\) is the expected number of replies per event, and \(\rho=0\) recovers the factorised model. Cells show test \(\ell\)/event, with the change versus \(\rho=0\) and the fitted \(\rho\) in brackets.

A temporal motif is a pair of events sharing a node: an event \(i\to j\) at time \(t\) followed by a second event within a window \(\delta\). The second event's source and target fall into exactly one of six types. Click a type to focus on it. In each diagram the dark arrow is the first event and the coloured arrow its follow-up. Rates are the share of events followed by that type within \(\delta\).

Which motifs are over-represented?

Observed rates in the training window against two simulated references: the featured model and a structure-only null (Poisson clock × the same mark layer, so degrees and blocks are kept but timing is random). The z-score is (observed − simulated mean) ÷ simulated s.d.

Over-represented (z > 2 vs the null): the motif appears more often than degrees and blocks alone imply, so it needs a temporal mechanism. Reproduced (|z| ≤ 2 vs the featured model): the model accounts for it.

Temporal networks as marked point processes

Events are pairs \((t_k, i_k\!\to\! j_k)\) on \([0,T]\). Each directed pair carries an intensity \(\lambda_{ij}(t)\), and the log-likelihood of a marked non-homogeneous Poisson process is

$$\ell(\{\lambda_{ij}\})=\sum_k \log\lambda_{i_kj_k}(t_k)-\sum_{(i,j)}\int_0^T\lambda_{ij}(t)\,dt .$$

Maximum-entropy factorisation

Maximising the path entropy \(-\sum_{ij}\int(\lambda_{ij}\log\lambda_{ij}-\lambda_{ij})\,dt\) subject to time-dependent constraints on partition totals \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E_r}\lambda_{ij}(t)=G_r(t)\) and time-independent linear constraints on the integrated intensities gives

$$\lambda_{ij}(t)=\varphi_{r(i,j)}(t)\,w_{ij},\qquad w_{ij}=M_{ij}\,e^{\Theta_{ij}+\theta^{\rm out}_i+\theta^{\rm in}_j+\zeta_{g(i)g(j)}}.$$

Stationarity of the Lagrangian gives \(-\log\lambda_{ij}(t)+\alpha_{r(i,j)}(t)+\Psi_{ij}=0\), since \(\partial_\lambda(\lambda\log\lambda-\lambda)=\log\lambda\); hence \(\varphi_r=e^{\alpha_r}\) and \(w_{ij}=e^{\Psi_{ij}}\). The likelihood then splits exactly into a time part and a mark part, \(\ell=\ell_{\rm time}[G]+\sum_k\log p_{m_k}\), so each layer can be fitted and compared independently.

Layers implemented here

  • Poisson and NHPP (piecewise constant, user-set bins) global profiles.
  • Hawkes-exp: \(G(t)=\mu+\eta\beta\sum_{t_k\lt t}e^{-\beta(t-t_k)}\), with branching ratio \(\eta\). Maximum likelihood by multi-start Nelder–Mead on \((\log\mu,\operatorname{logit}\eta,\log\beta)\), using the \(O(K)\) recursion over the \(K\) events.
  • Per-sender Poisson or Hawkes profiles, i.e. the partition \(E_r=\{(r,j)\}\). Here marks are conditional, \(p_{j\mid i}\).
  • Uniform: \(w_{ij}\equiv\text{const}\). Edge-Poisson: \(w_{ij}=N_{ij}\).
  • ME-Strength: the directed weighted configuration model \(w_{ij}=x_iy_j\), fitted by iterative proportional fitting to in/out strengths.
  • Block-DWCM: \(w_{ij}=x_iy_jz_{g(i)g(j)}\) on the full support, which also matches block-pair totals \(C_{ab}\). Blocks come from Louvain on the symmetrised training counts.
  • Block-Mask: the same constraints restricted to the support observed in training, \(M_{ij}=\mathbb 1[N_{ij}>0]\). The mask is selected from data and is not penalised in AIC.

For out-of-sample evaluation every mark distribution is mixed with the uniform one, \(p\to(1-\kappa)p+\kappa/|S|\), so that pairs unseen in training have finite likelihood. Parameter counts are for the normalised mark distribution: DWCM has \(a_{\rm out}+a_{\rm in}-2\) free parameters (one gauge, one normalisation; \(a\) = number of active senders/receivers), block factors add the non-empty block cells minus \(2B-1\) gauges, and conditional marks drop the \(a_{\rm out}-1\) sender marginals now carried by the time layer. Per-sender time layers give senders with no training events a regularising rate \(0.5/T\) (not counted as a parameter), so test likelihoods stay finite. A Hawkes fit whose maximum is not above the Poisson likelihood, or whose kernel is longer than half the training window (\(\beta T<2\)), is reported in the Poisson limit \(\eta=0\).

Reciprocity

The factorised models cannot express that a message \(i\to j\) makes a reply \(j\to i\) more likely soon after. The extension adds a reply kernel to every pair,

$$\lambda_{ij}(t)=c\,G_{r(i,j)}(t)\,p_{ij}+\rho\,\beta_\rho\!\!\sum_{t_k\lt t,\;m_k=j\to i}\!\!e^{-\beta_\rho(t-t_k)},$$

i.e. a superposition of the MaxEnt process with a reply cascade. Every event spawns on average \(\rho\) replies on the reversed pair, with delays \(\sim{\rm Exp}(\beta_\rho)\). Replies feed back into the global Hawkes excitation, and replies to replies are allowed. The model is stable if \(c\,\eta+\rho<1\) (per-sender: \(c\max_i\eta_i+\rho<1\)). The log-likelihood keeps an \(O(K)\) recursion along pair chains,

$$\ell=\sum_k\log\!\Big(c\,G(t_k)\,p_{m_k}+\rho\beta_\rho R_{m_k}(t_k)\Big)-c\!\int\! G\,dt-\rho\sum_k\big(1-e^{-\beta_\rho(T-t_k)}\big),$$

where \(R_{ij}(t)\) is the decayed count of past \(j\to i\) events. With the global Hawkes layer, \((\mu,\eta,\beta,\rho,\beta_\rho)\) are fitted jointly by maximum likelihood. For the other layers the fitted time profile is kept and \((c,\rho,\beta_\rho)\) are profiled, so those fits are conditional on the first-stage estimate. Simulations use the exact branching representation. On a generator whose only mark effect is replying, the estimator recovers \(\rho\), \(\beta_\rho\), \(\eta\) and \(\beta\) without detectable bias. When other mark effects are present (repeats, broadcasts), \(\rho\) absorbs part of them.

The additive form leaves the time × mark factorisation, but keeps the mark layer's MaxEnt weights and has a branching interpretation. The strict maximum-entropy alternative constrains \(\mathbb E\int\lambda_{ij}h_{ij}\,dt\) with \(h_{ij}(t)=\sum_{m_k=j\to i}e^{-\beta_\rho(t-t_k)}\). It gives a log-linear coupling \(\lambda_{ij}=\varphi(t)\,w_{ij}\,e^{\theta h_{ij}(t)}\), whose compensator needs numerical integration and which can explode for \(\theta>0\).

How the models are fitted

  • Blocks: Louvain modularity maximisation (resolution \(\gamma\)) on the symmetrised training counts.
  • Mark layers: closed form (Uniform, Edge-Poisson) or iterative proportional fitting, which alternately rescales rows, columns and block cells of \(w\) until every constraint is met to a relative error of \(10^{-7}\).
  • Time layers: closed form (Poisson, NHPP) or maximum likelihood by multi-start Nelder–Mead on transformed parameters, with an \(O(K)\) likelihood recursion (Hawkes).
  • Reciprocity: joint maximum likelihood of all five parameters for the global Hawkes layer; for the other layers a profile fit of a scale \(c\), \(\rho\) and \(\beta_\rho\) on top of the fitted time profile.

The Fitting tab shows each step, its iterations and convergence on the current data.

Temporal motifs

For an event \(i\to j\) at \(t\), its first follow-up of type \(m\) is the earliest later event whose (source, target) is \((i,j)\) for repetition, \((j,i)\) for reciprocity, \((i,k)\) for broadcast, \((k,j)\) for convergence, \((j,k)\) for chain or \((k,i)\) for reverse chain, with \(k\notin\{i,j\}\). The motif rate is

$$r_m(\delta)=\frac{\#\{k:\ t_k+\delta\le T,\ d_m(k)\le\delta\}}{\#\{k:\ t_k+\delta\le T\}},$$

where \(d_m(k)\) is the delay to that follow-up. Only events whose whole window fits inside the data count. Models are compared through simulations: the featured model, a structure-only null (Poisson clock with the same marks), and forecasts on the test window. Forecasts are exact for these linear models: each training event still to fire contributes \(\mathrm{Poisson}(\eta e^{-\beta(T_s-t_k)})\) offspring after the split, at delays \(\mathrm{Exp}(\beta)\), and likewise for replies.

Diagnostics

  • Time rescaling: \(\Delta\Lambda_k=\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_k}G\,dt\) are i.i.d. Exp(1) under the true model, so a KS test on \(1-e^{-\Delta\Lambda_k}\) checks the time layer. Parameters are estimated on the same data, so the reported p-values are conservative (in tests with a fitted Poisson rate, about 0.5% rejections at nominal 5%): a rejection is meaningful, an acceptance is weak evidence.
  • Unique edges and degrees under Poissonisation: \(\mathbb E[U]=\sum_{i\ne j}(1-e^{-Kp_{ij}})\).
  • Motifs are compared against simulations of the fitted model and of the same mark layer with a Poisson clock, isolating the role of temporal clustering.

Adding datasets to this page

Any whitespace-, comma- or semicolon-separated edge list with columns source target timestamp works; headers and lines starting with # or % are skipped, and ISO dates are parsed. Visitors can upload files directly. To offer datasets from this site, put files in the repository (e.g. data/enron.csv) and list them at the top of the page script:

const HOSTED_DATASETS = [
  {name: 'Enron e-mail', url: 'data/enron.csv'},
  {name: 'SocioPatterns hospital', url: 'data/hospital.csv'},
];
