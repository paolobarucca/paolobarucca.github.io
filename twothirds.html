Two-thirds of the average

Everybody picks a whole number from 0 to 100. The winner is whoever comes closest to two-thirds of the average. Play it twice and watch the target retreat.

Why the answer keeps moving

The ladder

Each step assumes everybody else reasoned one step less, and multiplies by two-thirds.

  1. Pick at random: the average is about 50.
  2. So aim for two-thirds of 50: 33.
  3. But everyone can work that out: 22.
  4. And that too: 15, then 10, then 7 …
  5. All the way down to 0, the only answer nobody can improve on. (With whole numbers, everybody saying 1 is stable too.)

Almost nobody plays 0, and anybody who does loses. The winning number is a fact about the room, not about the game. In a second round everybody knows roughly what the room did, aims lower, and so lowers the target: the equilibrium is where the crowd is heading, not where it is.

Where it started: Jeux & Stratégie, 1981

Alain Ledoux used the game as a tie-break for 4,078 readers of his French puzzle magazine, asking for a positive whole number below a billion.
